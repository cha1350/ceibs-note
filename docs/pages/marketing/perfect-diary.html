Perfect Diary: From KOL Growth To Sustainable Beauty Platform

Perfect Diary grew extremely fast by using a digitally native, KOL-powered direct-to-consumer model to win young Chinese beauty consumers. By 2021, Yatsen had to decide whether this model could remain defensible as influencer marketing became crowded, expensive, and easy for domestic and global competitors to copy.

1. First-Pass Thesis

Use in your own voice
One-sentence answer Perfect Diary's original KOL-driven model was the right launch strategy for a Chinese DTC cosmetics brand, but Yatsen's next challenge is to convert traffic-driven growth into a sustainable multi-brand beauty business with stronger product credibility, retention, and customer lifetime value.

The case is less about whether influencer marketing works. It clearly worked. The deeper issue is whether a company can keep capturing value after the channel that created early growth becomes expensive and widely copied.

2. Core Managerial Question

Decision problem

The central question is not simply, "Should Yatsen spend more on KOLs?" It is:

Managerial question How can Yatsen transform from a fast-growing KOL-driven color cosmetics brand into a sustainable multi-brand beauty company with stronger product credibility, repeat purchase, and customer lifetime value?

KOL Reliance

Should Perfect Diary continue relying heavily on KOL marketing after the channel becomes more competitive?

Skincare Expansion

Should Yatsen expand from color cosmetics into skincare, where the market is larger but requires stronger R&D credibility?

Owned Advantage

Can Yatsen turn social followers, WeChat communities, customer data, and analytics into a deeper advantage than paid influencer traffic?

3. Story Of The Case

What happened
2016

Yatsen Holding was founded by Jinfeng Huang and his cofounders as a new Chinese beauty company.

2017

Perfect Diary launched as a direct-to-consumer color cosmetics brand targeting young Chinese women with fashionable, affordable products.

2018-2019

The brand built traction through Taobao, Tmall, RED, WeChat, Douyin, and KOL content. It opened its first offline store in Guangzhou in January 2019.

2020

Perfect Diary became the leading domestic color cosmetics brand and nearly caught Maybelline in China's total color cosmetics market.

2021

By Q1 2021, Yatsen had 245 physical stores and 9.6 million DTC customers, but marketing expenses and losses raised questions about the sustainability of the model.

Perfect Diary's rise came from tight fit between target customers, digital channels, content, and KOL marketing. Young consumers were social-media-native, responsive to beauty tutorials, and open to domestic brands. The company discovered that RED posts about color testing and product recommendations were linked to search and sales, then built a repeatable KOL engine around content, engagement tracking, social buzz, and Tmall conversion.

The problem was that the same playbook became common. Domestic competitors such as Florasis copied and refined influencer marketing, while global incumbents such as L'Oreal, Estee Lauder, and P&G invested heavily in KOLs and livestreaming. The early growth engine remained powerful, but less distinctive and more expensive.

5. 5C Analysis

Analysis layer

Customers

Young Chinese women, especially Gen Z and millennials, were digital-first, trend-sensitive, and responsive to beauty content from KOLs. They bought not only functional cosmetics, but also identity, experimentation, and social confidence.

Exhibits 6, 8, and 9 show why KOLs fit this segment: content quality, celebrity endorsement, and influencer recommendations mattered strongly in cosmetics.

Company

Yatsen's strengths were data-driven KOL operations, social commerce, fast product development, in-house engineers, a large follower base, and WeChat communities.

The weakness was profitability. Exhibits 1a and 1b show rapid growth paired with high marketing spending and widening losses.

Competitors

Global players had R&D, brand heritage, and large budgets. Domestic brands copied influencer playbooks. Top KOLs became powerful partners who could capture value through fees, commissions, and discounts.

Collaborators

Collaborators included KOLs, MCNs, RED, Weibo, Douyin, WeChat, Tmall, JD.com, ODM partners, co-branding partners, research institutions, and acquired skincare brands.

Context

China's beauty market was growing, online shopping penetration was rising, social commerce was normalizing, and domestic brands were gaining acceptance. Later, the context changed as KOL costs rose and competitors crowded into the same channels.

6. STP Strategy

Strategy layer

Segmentation

Yatsen segmented by age, life stage, beauty need, identity, and price tier. Perfect Diary focused on fashionable, affordable color cosmetics. Little Ondine, Pink Bear, Abby's Choice, Galenic, Dr. Wu, and Eve Lom addressed different identities and price positions.

Targeting

The original target was young, digital-native Chinese women who wanted beauty experimentation but had limited budgets. As this target became crowded, Yatsen needed a portfolio logic to reach different customers and increase lifetime value.

Positioning

Perfect Diary positioned itself as fashionable, affordable, high value-for-money beauty made for Chinese consumers. At the corporate level, Yatsen needed to position itself as a digitally native multi-brand beauty company with consumer insight, community, and product credibility.

7. 4P Analysis

Execution layer

Product

Perfect Diary used fast product development, SKU variety, KOL testing, and tutorial-friendly designs. Color cosmetics rewarded speed and trend sensitivity. Skincare, however, required efficacy, ingredients, safety, and trust.

Place / Channels

The brand started online through Taobao and Tmall, then expanded across RED, WeChat, Douyin, JD.com, and offline stores. Exhibit 7 shows why a multi-platform approach was necessary.

Promotion

Promotion was the heart of the case. Perfect Diary built a KOL operating system: identify KOLs, track engagement, connect content to Tmall search and sales, and use KOL feedback for product launches. Exhibits 10 and 11 show why this advantage became less sustainable.

Price

Perfect Diary's price logic was value-for-money. Livestreaming helped conversion but increased discount pressure. Premium skincare brands could capture higher margins only if credibility supported the price.

8. Deep Summary By Theme

Discussion lens

Product-market-channel fit

Perfect Diary matched a fast-growing consumer segment with the right channels and message. Young consumers were already spending time on social media and shopping online. They wanted tutorials, peer-like advice, and products that felt fashionable but affordable.

Data made KOL marketing systematic

Perfect Diary tracked KOL performance, engagement, fan growth, and links to Tmall search and sales. The company used different KOL tiers for reach, credibility, sustained interest, and conversion.

Product and promotion were integrated

KOLs were used before launch, during launch, and after launch. Tutorials converted uncertainty into confidence, especially for bold colors and palettes that required education.

The model became vulnerable

The initial advantage looked like channel arbitrage. Once competitors learned the playbook and KOL prices rose, Yatsen needed stronger brand equity, customer data, retention, and product credibility.

KOLs captured value too

Top KOLs helped create demand but also captured value through slot fees, commissions, and exclusive discounts. The question became who owned the customer and who kept the profit.

Skincare is different

Skincare was larger and more attractive, but strategically different. Customers cared more about formula, evidence, safety, and long-term efficacy. This explains Yatsen's acquisitions of skincare brands with greater credibility.

9. Marketing Process Framework

Course framework
Analysis: 5C

Customers were young, digital, and influencer-responsive. The company was strong in data, DTC, and KOL operations but weak in profitability. Competitors copied digital tactics or had stronger R&D and brand heritage. Collaborators, especially KOLs and platforms, were powerful but costly. Context favored social commerce but later made the channel less distinctive.

Strategy: STP

Perfect Diary's original STP was clear: young Chinese women, affordable trend-driven cosmetics, social commerce. Yatsen's next stage required multiple STPs across brands, with skincare and higher-LTV customers becoming more important.

Execution: 4P

Product needed to move from fast SKU launches to hero products and skincare efficacy. Place needed to combine platform commerce, owned communities, and offline experience. Promotion needed more selective KOL use and better ROI discipline. Price needed to avoid discount addiction while supporting premium positions where credibility allowed.

Outcomes

The desired outcome was not just customer acquisition, which Perfect Diary had already proven. The next goal was stronger retention, higher customer lifetime value, and improved profitability.

10. Survey-Ready Position

Draft from understanding
Position Perfect Diary's original KOL-driven model was the right launch strategy for a DTC cosmetics brand in China, but it is no longer sufficient as the company's main competitive advantage.

The model worked because young beauty consumers trusted KOLs, cared about content quality, and shopped heavily online. Exhibits 6, 8, and 9 support this customer-channel fit. However, Exhibits 1a, 1b, 10, and 11 show that growth became expensive, KOL competition intensified, and profitability suffered.

Yatsen should evolve from a traffic-driven beauty startup into a multi-brand beauty company built around customer data, community, product quality, and scientific credibility. KOLs should remain important, but should be used selectively for product education, launch testing, and content creation rather than as the only growth engine.

The expansion into skincare is strategically attractive because the market is much larger and higher margin, but Yatsen should rely on acquired brands and R&D partnerships to build trust instead of assuming that the Perfect Diary playbook will work unchanged.

Recommended answer angle

  • Pursue skincare and multi-brand expansion.
  • Reduce dependence on paid KOL acquisition.
  • Use KOLs for education, testing, and launch storytelling.
  • Build retention through WeChat communities, customer data, and cross-brand customer journeys.
  • Strengthen product credibility through hero products, R&D, clinical or ingredient proof, and brand-specific positioning.
