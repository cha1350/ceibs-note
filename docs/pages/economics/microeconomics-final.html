Your quick reference

Market failure and externalitiesMSC = MPC + marginal external damage. Efficient output satisfies MSB = MSC. A corrective tax equals marginal external damage at efficient output in the standard competitive model.
Five externality approachesTaxes/subsidies; private bargaining; legal rules (injunction or liability); sale/auction of rights; direct regulation.
Tax and subsidy graphsWith an external cost, MSC lies above MPC and private output is too high. Tax: buyer price − seller net price = tax. With an external benefit, MSB lies above MPB and private output is too low. Subsidy: seller receipt − buyer price = subsidy.
Information problemsAdverse selection: hidden characteristics. Moral hazard: hidden actions and changed incentives. Search while expected marginal benefit exceeds marginal cost. Warranties, certification, reputation and contract design can help; governments can also face information and incentive problems.
First or second mover?Use the actual information, feasible actions, and credible commitments. Moving first can help or hurt; observation can benefit the second mover.
Payoff matrixFirst number = row player; second = column player. Compare the row player's payoffs vertically and the column player's horizontally.
DominanceA strictly dominant action is better against every possible opponent action. Check every comparison.
Sequential gameStart at the last decision. Choose what that player actually prefers, then work backward. Test threats after the triggering event.
Price customizationIdentify groups or self-selection; check resale. With the same serving cost, less price-sensitive customers can support a higher price when groups can be separated.
Repeated interactionFuture punishment can support cooperation if it is credible and the future matters enough. A known finite prisoners' dilemma has different incentives.

For short answers: state the rule, apply the numbers, then explain the result.
