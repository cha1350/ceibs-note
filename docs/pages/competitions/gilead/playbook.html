What Judges Likely Value

Cross-year judging logic reconstructed from public prompts, rules, organizer quotes, and winner reflections.

Grounded Originality

Prompts repeatedly say there is no single right answer. The best teams propose novel choices, but tie them to case data, constraints, assumptions, and implementation.

Executive Logic

Gilead's 2020 organizer quote singled out "logic" and full problem coverage. Later prompts ask for KPIs, budget rationale, roadmaps, and contingency plans.

Q&A Survival

Finalist formats are short and probing. Teams need each member fluent enough to defend assumptions, tradeoffs, risk mitigation, and stakeholder impacts.

Preparation Roadmap

A practical plan for competing, based on the public record and what finalist prompts reward.

Build A Pharma Strategy Spine Before Case Drop

Prepare templates for launch readiness, clinical trial site selection, market access, revenue diversification, ESG scoring, China entry, and M&A integration. Know the recurring vocabulary: LOE, PTRS, TPP, HEOR, KOL, COGS, reimbursement, GCP, site activation, patient funnel, and risk-adjusted NPV.

Make The First 12 Hours Count

Read the prompt once for facts, once for decision asks, and once for exhibits. Convert it into a one-page issue tree, an assumptions log, and a "must answer" checklist. Do not build slides until the recommendation logic is coherent.

Score Options Transparently

Winning public examples use matrices, budgets, and weighted frameworks. Keep the math simple enough to defend: five to eight criteria, explicit weights, sensitivity checks, and a clear reason why the selected option beats the runner-up.

Turn Strategy Into Operating Cadence

Judges will probe feasibility. Show 30/60/90-day actions, year-by-year milestones, budget owners, functional dependencies, launch gates, risk triggers, and contingency moves.

Practice Q&A Like A Board Meeting

Assign one teammate to challenge every slide. Prepare answers for: why this option, why now, what could fail, what if your top assumption is wrong, what would you cut, what would you do with more budget, and how patients benefit.
