01 / ID

What kind of company is Gilead?

Gilead is a research-based biopharmaceutical company with a concentrated commercial core and a deliberately expanding portfolio.

Core thesis

An HIV franchise funding the construction of a broader biopharma company.

Gilead's current economics are dominated by HIV, especially Biktarvy. Its future strategy is a barbell: defend and extend that unusually strong franchise with long-acting medicines, while using internal R&D, partnerships, and acquisitions to build durable growth engines in oncology and inflammation.

This makes Gilead financially powerful but strategically exposed. The same franchise that provides funding also creates concentration risk and raises the standard for every diversification investment.

What it is not

  • Not a diversified consumer-health conglomerate.
  • Not only an HIV company, although HIV remains the commercial center.
  • Not purely an internal-discovery model; external innovation is central.
  • Not a single manufacturing model: pills, injectables, biologics, ADCs, and personalized CAR T require different systems.

02 / MODEL

How Gilead creates and captures value

The company does not simply invent a drug and sell it. Value must survive six linked systems, each with a different failure mode.

01 DISCOVER

Find a mechanism

Internal science identifies targets, molecules, delivery systems, or cell-therapy approaches.

02 SOURCE

Buy or partner

Licensing, collaborations, options, and acquisitions add programs and platforms from outside.

03 PROVE

Run clinical trials

Evidence must establish safety, efficacy, patient fit, and comparative value. Most trial activity is outsourced to CROs.

04 APPROVE

Secure the label

Regulators determine the population, indication, dosing, warnings, and evidence obligations that define commercial potential.

05 DELIVER

Manufacture and launch

Tablets, injections, ADCs, and made-to-order CAR T therapies need very different supply and treatment-site networks.

06 REALIZE

Win access and use

Physicians, patients, payers, health systems, and distributors determine real uptake, net price, and persistence.

Revenue logic

The list price is not the revenue.

Gilead records product sales after rebates, chargebacks, co-pay support, prompt-pay discounts, distributor fees, and returns. In 2025, those deductions totaled $20.0 billion, or 41% of gross product sales. A case that models only volume times list price will overstate economics.

Strategic implication

Commercial success is a system design problem.

Evidence, label, payer policy, provider workflow, manufacturing capacity, patient support, and channel inventory all interact. For Gilead, a scientifically strong idea is incomplete until the access and delivery architecture works.

07 / ARC

The strategic arc

Gilead's history is a sequence of concentrated scientific bets, several of which reshaped the company.

1987

Founded

Michael Riordan founded Gilead, initially building an antiviral-focused biotechnology company.

2001-2004

HIV foundation

Viread and then Truvada helped establish Gilead as an antiviral leader and supported combination-regimen strategy.

2011-2013

Pharmasset to Sovaldi

The roughly $11B Pharmasset purchase brought the compound that became sofosbuvir, transforming HCV treatment and Gilead's economics.

2017

Kite, $11.9B

Gilead entered cell therapy and gained the platform that became its Kite oncology business.

2018

Biktarvy

FDA approval launched the single-tablet regimen that became Gilead's largest product.

2020

Immunomedics, ~$21B

Trodelvy became the cornerstone of the solid-tumor oncology strategy.

2024-2025

Livdelzi + Yeztugo

CymaBay added Livdelzi; Yeztugo opened a new long-acting HIV prevention era.

2026

Platform buying

Arcellx, Tubulis, and Ouro expanded cell therapy, ADC, and autoimmune capabilities with $11.3B of first-half acquisition cash outflow.

Pattern

Gilead is willing to pay early for concentrated scientific conviction.

Pharmasset became a landmark success; other deals have produced mixed outcomes and impairments. The lesson is not that acquisitions are good or bad. It is that Gilead repeatedly uses its cash flow and development capabilities to convert external science into new franchises, accepting large binary risks.

08 / ACCESS

Access is part of the operating model

For infectious disease, scientific impact depends on reach. Access strategy is therefore both mission-critical and reputation-critical.

Current example

Lenacapavir access planning started before global launch.

Gilead signed royalty-free voluntary licenses with six manufacturers covering 120 primarily low- and lower-middle-income countries, completed technology transfers, and paired this with government and multilateral partnerships.

The strategic tension remains: the company must fund innovation, protect incentives, manage manufacturing and quality, and demonstrate that breakthrough efficacy translates into broad, timely, affordable access.

Why Gilead emphasizes access

Its HIV and viral hepatitis medicines operate in diseases with large global burdens, significant inequality, stigma, public funding, and strong civil-society involvement.

Historical trust issue

A 2015 U.S. Senate investigation criticized Sovaldi's $84,000 launch price and concluded the pricing strategy prioritized revenue, intensifying scrutiny of affordability and payer access.

09 / RISK

Strategic tensions to watch

These are the recurring constraints a serious recommendation must acknowledge and manage.

High structural exposure

HIV concentration

HIV generated 71.8% of 2025 product sales; Biktarvy alone supplied almost half. A shift in treatment standards, pricing, competition, or share would matter disproportionately.

Lifecycle

Patent and exclusivity clocks

Patents are time-limited and challengeable. Biktarvy U.S. generic settlements currently point to April 2036 for full-dose entry, while other products face earlier clocks.

Binary evidence

Clinical and regulatory risk

Programs may miss endpoints, face safety findings, be placed on hold, receive narrow labels, or never secure approval. Pipeline count cannot neutralize program-level failure.

Capital allocation

Acquisition execution

Large deals can accelerate strategy but also create integration cost, impairment, and overpayment risk. The 2024 Immunomedics-related impairment is a visible warning.

Commercial system

Payer and pricing pressure

Rebates, Medicare redesign, government negotiation, most-favored-nation policies, and payer mix can reduce realized price even when prescription demand grows.

Delivery

Manufacturing complexity

Small molecules, sterile injectables, ADCs, and individualized cell therapies have distinct supply, quality, lead-time, and capacity risks.

Market

Competition

Products compete on efficacy, safety, convenience, resistance, physician acceptance, reimbursement, and price. Q2 2026 cell-therapy sales fell 14% amid competitive headwinds.

Stakeholder

Access and reputation

For high-impact infectious-disease products, slow or unequal access can erode trust with patients, advocates, governments, and global health partners.

Portfolio tradeoff

Focus versus optionality

Too much concentration creates fragility; too many programs dilute capital and leadership attention. Gilead must prune while preserving enough shots on goal.

12 / TERMS

Fast glossary

Useful language for reading filings and discussing biopharma strategy.

ADC: antibody-drug conjugate

An antibody linked to a cytotoxic payload, designed to deliver the drug preferentially to cells expressing a target. Trodelvy is an ADC.

CAR T: chimeric antigen receptor T-cell therapy

A patient's T cells are collected, engineered to recognize a cancer target, manufactured, and infused back. It is both a medicine and a complex service/supply-chain model.

PrEP: pre-exposure prophylaxis

Medicine taken or administered before possible HIV exposure to reduce acquisition risk.

Gross-to-net

The difference between gross product sales and net reported sales after rebates, chargebacks, discounts, assistance, fees, and returns.

IPR&D: in-process research and development

Research and development assets acquired before they have completed development. Acquisitions and impairments can create large GAAP expenses.

PBC / HBV / HCV / HDV

Primary biliary cholangitis; hepatitis B virus; hepatitis C virus; and hepatitis delta virus. Each has a different disease mechanism, patient journey, and treatment market.
